Service customers are drifting, and the FTC is reading dealer ads.

  • 54%of owners with cars two years old or newer came back to the dealership for service in 2025, down from 72% in 2023, in Cox Automotive’s service study.1
  • 97dealerships got FTC warning letters about advertising and pricing in March 2026. Their names were made public on May 28, 2026.2

Dealer lawyers now say a salesperson posting from a personal phone is still acting for the store,3 and trade press warns that offers or payments posted there can go out without the disclosures they need.4

No payments, and your store’s name on every post.

  • Payments and loan terms When an ad mentions a monthly payment, a down payment or a loan term, it has to give the full credit terms too (Truth in Lending, 12 CFR 1026.24).5 Loop31 stops a post like that until the terms are there or the payment comes out.
  • Your dealership’s name When an employee posts about the store’s cars, the post has to show they work there (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.5).6 Loop31 puts your dealership’s name on the picture and under the caption of every post, and it can’t be turned off.
  • Nothing to sell The ready-made posts are friendly “think of me” posts, like the penny test for tire tread or a favorite fall drive. None of them quotes a price or a payment, and none carries a carmaker’s logo.

What you see as their manager.

You see

  • each salesperson’s name, and that their seat is paid
  • when they joined, and the date of their last post
  • which Loops they have on (you can’t change them)
  • the posts that went out from the day they joined, with dates and captions (captions for 30 days)

You never see

  • drafts, or posts they skipped
  • their photos or the vehicles they’ve added
  • their phone number or setup answers
  • anyone who pays for Loop31 on their own
