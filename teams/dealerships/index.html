Most sold customers buy their next car somewhere else.

  • 37.9%of returning buyers bought from the same dealer at Subaru, which won S&P Global Mobility’s 2025 award for loyalty to the dealer. Even at the best brand, most went somewhere else.1
  • 1 in 5buyers never heard from the dealership after the sale, in a 2013 Maritz study of more than 4,200 buyers. The longer they went without hearing, the less likely they were to buy there again.2

Joe Girard sold 13,001 cars at one Chevrolet store and sent every customer a card every month.3 Loop31 is that habit for your whole floor, every week, on the phone your customers already check. His story.

Ready posts, and the cars on your lot.

  • Friendly posts every week Each salesperson gets 4 ready posts a week by default, up to one a day: car care tips, customer reviews, their part of town. They look them over in about a minute and share from their own Facebook and Instagram in about 20 seconds.
  • New arrivals from your website A manager connects your dealership’s website once and confirms the store owns it and its photos. Each night, Loop31 reads the cars on it.
  • One salesperson at a time Each new car is offered to one salesperson. They claim it, or pass it to the next. The post uses the car’s details and your own photos, with no price or payment in it.
  • Nothing goes out without them The salesperson OKs every post before it’s shared. Car posts are at most a third of their week, so the friendly posts still lead.

Who posted, this week and this month.

The team portal counts each salesperson’s posts from the day they join, like the board you already keep for units. When someone has a post due today that isn’t posted, send a reminder: one a day and three a week per person, between 9 AM and 7 PM their time. It shows at the top of their Home screen in the app.

You never see their drafts, their photos, their phone number, or anything they haven’t posted. The full list.

No payments, and your store’s name on every post.

  • 97dealership groups got FTC warning letters about advertising and pricing in March 2026. Their names were made public on May 28, 2026.4

Dealer lawyers now say a salesperson posting from a personal phone is still acting for the store,5 and trade press warns that offers or payments posted there can go out without the disclosures they need.6

  • Payments and loan terms When an ad mentions a monthly payment, a down payment or a loan term, it has to give the full credit terms too (Truth in Lending, 12 CFR 1026.24).7 Loop31 stops a post like that until the terms are there or the payment comes out.
  • Your dealership’s name When an employee posts about the store’s cars, the post has to show they work there (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.5).8 Loop31 puts your dealership’s name on the picture and under the caption of every post, and it can’t be turned off. You can add your store’s logo and one line too.
  • No prices or payments from us Most posts are friendly “think of me” posts, like the penny test for tire tread or a favorite fall drive. The posts Loop31 writes never quote a price or a payment, and none carries a carmaker’s logo.
  • The check record For each post that goes out, the portal keeps which checks ran and what they flagged: “nothing flagged”, “a warning” or “a rule”, never the words. Export it for any dates as a spreadsheet.

These checks catch common problems. They aren’t legal advice.

Who approves it, and who runs it

  • The general manager or dealer principal approves it Every post carries your store’s name, so it’s your call. A dealer association’s sample social media policy says staff can’t post the dealership’s trademark or logo “without express written permission”.13 Loop31 is a managed way to give it: you approve the pilot by name, you choose who holds a seat, the store’s name goes on every post, and you can read every post that went out.
  • The GSM runs it on the floor Make your general sales manager an admin in the portal. They see who posted this week, send the reminders, and bring the numbers to the sales meeting.
  • Your marketing or digital director Where your store or group has one, they own the website and social media. The posts go on each salesperson’s own profile, never the store’s page, and the owner can add your logo and one line to every post. As an admin, they can connect the website for new arrivals and export the check record.

Questions general managers ask

Will my salespeople actually use it?
It’s designed to take about a minute a week, and their phone reminds them when it’s time to post. You see who posted, and you can send a reminder to anyone whose post is due today. Seat only the people who want it, and try it free for 30 days with your own floor.
Our OEM sets the rules for our marketing.
These posts go on the salesperson’s own profile, not the store’s page. The ready-made posts carry no carmaker logos, and no prices or payments.
I can’t have salespeople posting off-brand.
The posts are friendly, and the ones Loop31 writes carry no prices or payments. Each one is checked before it can be shared, and you can read every post that went out, with what the checks said.
What happens when someone leaves the floor?
NADA’s 2021 study put sales consultant turnover at 67% a year.12 Take their seat back in the portal and give it to the next hire. They keep their own Facebook and Instagram, as they would anyway; while they’re with you, their posts name your store.
Do you get our customer list?
No. Loop31 doesn’t connect to your CRM or DMS and never reads a customer’s information. From your website it reads only the cars you publish. More on the security and data page.
We’re part of a dealer group.
