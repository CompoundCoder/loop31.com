Privacy policy

Last updated October 24, 2026

This page says what the Loop31 app collects, why, where it is kept, who sees it, and how to delete it. It is written for the people who use the app, not for lawyers. If anything is unclear, write to support@loop31.com.

Loop31 is made by Compound Coder LLC, an Indiana company. "We" and "us" mean Compound Coder LLC. "You" means the person using the app.

The short version

  • We keep what the app needs to do its job: your email, the business details that go under your posts, your posts and photos, your schedule, and the record of what you approved.
  • Everything is kept on our server in the United States, run for us by Supabase.
  • Caption text goes to an outside AI company only if you say yes in the app, and only caption text. Your name, phone, email, photos, and client reviews never go.
  • We never sell your data, show ads, or track you across other apps and sites.
  • You can delete your account in the app at any time, and everything we hold for you goes with it.

What we collect, and why

Your account

  • Email address. You sign in with a 6-digit code sent to it; there is no password. We also use it for support. It is kept by our sign-in service (Supabase Auth) and not copied into our own tables.
  • A sign-in ID, which ties your data to you, plus the sign-in code while it works (10 minutes). Supabase Auth keeps a log of sign-ins, including the IP address and a description of the device, to keep accounts safe.
  • Your name, typed at setup, for the line the app adds under every post.

Your business details

  • Work phone, brokerage or dealership, license state, Instagram handle, Facebook Page name, and the area you serve. They make up the line under every post and tell the app which state's advertising rules to apply.
  • Your setup answers: your line of work, the two questions at setup, the tone you picked, where you post, whether your Instagram is public, your time zone, the day and time you check next week's posts (Check posts), and how long setup took.
  • Your writing-help answer, kept as a record: yes or no, when you answered, and which wording you saw. That record stays on our server; it is never sent anywhere.

Your posts

  • Captions, the versions waiting for your OK, and the words in each caption that must stay exact.
  • Photos and videos. When you pick a photo, the app makes one cleaned copy on your phone (location data removed, no larger than 4,096 pixels on the long side) and uploads only that copy to our private storage. We never see your photo library: Apple's picker shows us only what you pick. Videos up to 90 seconds.
  • Stock backgrounds: which of Loop31's own stock photos a post uses, and when, so the same photo rests a few weeks before it comes back. These are our own copies in our storage; picking one sends nothing to the photographer or the site it came from.
  • Client reviews: the client's exact words, the name they want shown, the date, and your confirmation that they said it's OK to share.
  • Listing and vehicle details for the posts that follow a listing or a car from arrival to sale: address, price, MLS number, beds and baths, dates, who took the photos; or VIN, stock number, price, mileage, and payment terms.

Your schedule and approvals

  • The schedule: which post goes out when, and where. Reminders are built from it on your phone.
  • Your Check posts answers (Looks good, Skip, Edit words), whether a post went out, your streak and awards.
  • A history of what was approved, when, and what the checks said at the time. It is your own record, and your broker's or dealership's if they ever need it.

The checks

  • Results of the rule checks: which rule fired, the highlighted words, and the rule's basis.
  • Wording the outside AI pointed out (Real Estate, only if writing help is on): the phrase, a one-sentence reason, and which model said so.
  • A record for each post you share: which checks ran and what they flagged, as each check's name and level, never the words.

Keeping the service running

  • Server logs: which request came in, when, and how long it took. Our logs never contain caption text or anything you typed. Supabase's own logs of requests to our project also record the IP address and device description.
  • Error reports from the app: the error, the app and iOS version, and the device model. No user ID and no caption text. Apple's TestFlight sends us crash reports during the beta as well.
  • Two timings for measuring whether the app is quick enough: how long setup took, and how long sharing a post took. There is no analytics or crash-reporting vendor in the app.

If you pick "Other" at setup

If you sell something other than homes or cars, setup asks what you sell. We keep your answer with your account, to show which line of work should get ready-made posts next.

Your website, if you give it to us

With "Other", you can give us your business's website (or type a few sentences about your business instead) so the app can suggest post ideas. First you confirm that it is your business's site and that you have the right to use what it says. Then:

  • We keep: the address, when you agreed and to which wording, whether the monthly re-read is on, and the ideas, facts and Loops the app suggested and you kept or fixed. What you typed about your business is kept so it can be used again.
  • We never keep the pages. Our server reads up to 10 pages of that one site, sends their words to OpenAI to write the suggestions (see "Who we share it with"), and keeps only a fingerprint of each page so it can tell next month whether it changed.
  • About once a month the server reads the site again, and the app asks you about anything new or changed. Turn this off, or remove your website, in Me.
  • It reads only the site you gave, never other sites, and skips any page the site asks bots to stay out of.

Cars from a dealership's website

When a dealership's manager connects the dealership's website to its Loop31 team, they confirm that the dealership owns the site and the photos on it and that its salespeople may use them. Each night Loop31's server reads that site's robots.txt, its sitemap and its vehicle pages, as the robot Loop31Bot, honouring robots.txt, one page at a time. From each vehicle page it keeps only the car's published details (VIN, year, make, model, trim, mileage, condition, the price shown, the page's address and up to three photos) and never the page's text. It sends new VINs to NHTSA's free VIN decoder to confirm the year, make and model. It keeps copies of the car photos, with their metadata removed, for the dealership and for the salesperson a car goes to. No customer's information is ever read or kept: only the cars the dealership itself publishes. A manager can remove the website at any time in the team's Settings, and nothing more is read.

Whose website it is. We rely on the word of whoever gives us a website that it's theirs to give, since we can't check who owns a site, and we never read social networks, marketplaces or listing sites (Facebook, Instagram, Etsy, Amazon, Zillow, Realtor.com, Cars.com, Autotrader and the like). If your website was given to us without your OK, write to support@loop31.com and we'll stop reading it and delete what we got from it. The terms say more.

If you're on a team

If a brokerage or dealership gives you a seat, its managers see what the app showed you before you joined: your name, that your seat is paid, which Loops are on, and the posts that go out from the day you joined, with their dates and captions. They can send you one reminder a day when a post is due. They never see your drafts, photos, your own listings, vehicles, or anything you haven't posted. On a brokerage team, if you add listings from the brokerage's website, its managers see how many, never which ones.

  • Your posting numbers: managers see how many posts you marked posted this week and this month, out of those planned so far, and in each of the 3 months before, with your last post and your weeks in a row. They are counted from your schedule; nothing new is kept for them.
  • A manager's reminder: when you have a post due today that's ready and not yet marked posted, the team's owner or an admin can send you a reminder, at most one a day and three a week, and only between 9 AM and 7 PM your time. It shows at the top of Home that day with the sender's first name, never the post's words or picture. We keep each reminder (who sent it, which post, your day, the time, and, once Lock Screen reminders arrive, how many of your phones it reached) for 35 days, so the limits work. A line in the sender's own account history records that they sent one. Today the reminder shows in the app only.
  • The check record: for each post you share, managers see which checks ran and what they flagged, as the check's name and level, never the words.
  • The Monday email: a team's owner and admins get an email each Monday with last week's numbers, unless they turn it off. It has the team's name and the names of the people on it with their counts, never captions or photos. See Resend under "Who we share it with".

When we help you

A few Loop31 staff can look up an account by its exact email address to help with support, billing or a deletion request. They see counts, dates, your plan, your team, and your writing-help answer; never your name, captions or photos. Every lookup is recorded.

Where it is kept

Everything above lives in one project on Supabase, in the United States (Amazon Web Services, Northern Virginia). Supabase runs the database, the sign-in service, the private storage for photos and videos, the small server programs the app talks to, and the logs. Supabase works for us under its data processing terms and does not use your data for anything of its own.

Some things stay on your phone only: the files for a post that is ready to share, the crops, the reminders, and the pictures the app saved to your Photos.

Who we share it with

We share your data with the companies that do a job for us, and only what that job needs. Nobody gets it for their own use.

Connected accounts ("Posted for you")

Loop31 does not post on your behalf today. When that feature arrives, it will work only for accounts that allow it (Instagram Business or Creator accounts and Facebook Pages), and only if you connect one. Then:

  • We keep the access tokens Meta gives us, the account ID and handle, and the permissions you granted. They are encrypted before they reach our database.
  • We use them for two things: to publish the posts you approved at the scheduled time, and to read how many people each post reached.
  • You can disconnect the account in the app at any time. We delete the tokens then, and when you delete your account.
  • Nothing of this applies to a personal account. We never hold a token, login, or password for one, and never post to one.

What we never do

  • No tracking: no advertising ID, no ad software, no data brokers, no following you across other companies' apps or sites. The app never asks the iPhone's tracking permission because it has nothing to track.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing your data for advertising. No ads.
  • No posting to personal Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok profiles, and no logins or passwords for them.
  • No photos, name, phone, email, contacts, or client reviews to any AI company, ever. Only caption text, only with your yes.
  • No reading your photo library. The app can only add to it.
  • No reading your clipboard. The app writes the caption to it and nothing else.
  • No biometric data. Approving from the Lock Screen uses your phone's own unlock, which the app never sees.
  • No logging of what you type. Our server logs hold codes, counts, and timings only.

Phone permissions

  • Photos, add only. Asked the first time the app prepares a post, so it can save the post's picture to your Photos, where it is the newest one when you post. The app cannot see, list, or delete anything else in your library.
  • Camera. Asked only if you tap Take a photo. The photo is handled like a picked one.
  • Notifications. Asked after you first check your posts, for reminders.
  • Never asked: location, contacts, microphone, calendar, Bluetooth, or tracking.

Deleting your account

In the app, open Me and tap Delete account. The app lists what will be removed. When you confirm, everything goes: your profile and business details, your Loops and posts, every photo and video in our storage, your schedule, approvals and history, the check results, the reminders your manager sent you, your writing-help record, your website's address and the ideas from it, and your sign-in itself. Any connected account's tokens are removed and, where Meta allows it, revoked. The last thing deleted is your sign-in, so a step that fails can be tried again while you are still signed in.

What stays: the pictures the app saved to your Photos (the app can add to Photos but cannot delete from it; remove them yourself if you like), and any post already published to your own accounts. Backups roll off within 30 days. The companies in "Who we share it with" follow their own retention; for OpenAI that is up to 30 days of safety logs.

If you can't open the app, or if you are not a user but your details were put into it (for example, a client whose review an agent shared), email support@loop31.com and we will remove the data. Write from the email address you signed in with, if you have an account, so we know it's you.

Features that are coming

These are planned and not in the app yet. Before any of them ships, this page will say exactly what it adds:

  • Posted for you, described above, which adds Meta and the connected-account tokens.
  • Paid plans through the App Store. Apple handles billing and we never see card details; a subscription service would hold your user ID and purchase receipts.
  • Lock Screen updates for Check posts, which use an Apple push token.
  • Your manager's reminder on your Lock Screen, delivered through Expo's push service (Expo makes the tools the app is built with), which needs a push token from your phone. No phone sends one yet, and no reminder is pushed.
  • Listing and vehicle lookups: an address sent to a property-data service to check a listing is yours, and a VIN sent to the US government's free vehicle decoder.

Children

Loop31 is for working adults and is not for anyone under 18. We do not knowingly collect data from a child. If you think a child has made an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

When we change this page, we update the date at the top. For a change that matters, such as a new company receiving your data or a new kind of data collected, we tell you in the app before it takes effect, and where the law requires it we ask you again.
